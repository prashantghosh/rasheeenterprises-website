Going green

Sustainability here is a manufacturing decision, not a label

Three things determine the environmental cost of a packaging tape: what is in the adhesive, what the backing is made of, and how much of it you were sold. We can speak to all three, because we coat and slit our own film. The fourth — selling you thickness you do not need — is the one most suppliers will not discuss.

The adhesive

Water-based acrylic, because the alternative has to be recovered

Solvent-based adhesives coat well and cost less per kilo. They also release volatile organic compounds during coating, which then need capture and incineration, and they leave a faint solvent smell inside a sealed carton for days. Our coating line runs water-based acrylic exclusively, so there is no solvent inventory on site and no recovery system to fail.

Material efficiency

The greenest roll is the one you did not need

Over-specifying micron is the most common waste in this industry, and it is invisible because the carton still arrives sealed. A 50 micron tape on a 3 kg mailer works perfectly — it just uses about 25 per cent more polypropylene than the 38 micron grade that would have held it, at a higher price, for no gain in burst strength at the seam.

Burst strength is a property of the carton, not the tape. Once the adhesive bond exceeds the fibreboard's own tear resistance, adding film thickness moves the failure point nowhere — the board gives way first. That threshold is what we specify against, which is why our recommendation is sometimes a thinner grade than you asked for.

Strapping

Polypropylene instead of steel, and it goes back into the stream

PP strapping is recyclable, roughly a tenth the weight of steel banding for the same pallet, and it will not rust-mark a crate or a fabric bale in monsoon transit. Where a load genuinely exceeds what PET can carry we will tell you to use steel — but on most crated freight it is specified out of habit.
